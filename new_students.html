Before you fly

Sort these out from home

ISSS immigration check-in

Every new international student completes the Immigration Check-in in UAtlas in two parts:

  • Pre-arrival — before you enter the US (includes the orientation fee).
  • Post-arrival — after you land: US address, I-94, emergency contact.

A missing check-in puts a hold on your record and blocks registration — and you need it before ISSS can help with your SSN.

Phone & SIM

All US airports have free Wi-Fi, so you can message your pickup person as soon as you land.

  • If your phone supports eSIM, buy a plan before you fly and switch it on at the airport.
  • Need a physical SIM? We can have one waiting — tick it on the form.
  • Popular: Mint Mobile (cheapest, most new students), Google Fi, Tello, H2O, Ultra, or the big three — Verizon, T-Mobile, AT&T.

Check your handset is unlocked and works on US networks.

Arrival & pickup

You'll land at Salt Lake City International (SLC). We'll do our best to have someone meet you — send us your flight details and stay in touch with your pickup person.

If we can't find a driver, use Uber or Lyft. They don't take cash, so carry a card that works internationally (a dual-currency card from your Bangladeshi bank).

Campus is about 20–25 minutes from the airport by car.

Health records

  • Proof of two MMR (measles, mumps, rubella) doses, in English — the U requires it.
  • Don't get a TB test at home: Utah only accepts TB tests done after you arrive.
  • As an F-1 student you're automatically enrolled in the student health insurance (TA/RAs get it subsidised).

Courses

Graduate students must register for at least 9 credits — it activates your UCard, counts as full-time for your visa, and is needed for the SSN letter.

Not sure what to take? Ask your advisor or grad coordinator; you can usually change courses for a few weeks after classes start.

Money & cards

  • Get a dual-currency (international) card from your Bangladeshi bank — for Uber, Airbnb and your first shopping.
  • Carry the rest as cash endorsed on your passport; declare it if it's over $10,000.
  • Plan for about two months of costs before pay settles in — use the calculator.
Money

How much should you bring?

Your first paycheck can take three to six weeks to arrive — longer if paperwork is late. Bring enough to cover a deposit, setting up, and about two months of living. Change the numbers to match your plans.

Your numbers

Defaults are for a single student sharing a 2-bedroom near campus (average rent ≈ $1,575, so ≈ $790 each — Zumper, Oct 2026).

Bring about

When does the first paycheck come? TA / RA

University of Utah pays graduate assistants twice a month: work from the 1st–15th is paid on the 22nd; work from the 16th–end of month is paid on the 7th of the next month. For a typical fall start on August 16:

Early Augyou land
Aug 16contract starts
Sep 7first pay — if HR paperwork is done
Sep 22then every 7th & 22nd

Rent for August and September, plus the deposit, is usually due before Sep 7. If your I-9 or direct deposit is late, the first payment slips to the next pay day — so plan for at least two months.

Carry part as cash endorsed on your passport and part on a dual-currency (international) card from your Bangladeshi bank. Deposit the cash in a US account in your first week — cash isn't welcome in many shops.
After you land

Your first two weeks

Do these roughly in order — each step unlocks the next. Tick them off as you go; ticks are saved on this device.

Finish the ISSS post-arrival check-in

In UAtlas → Requests → Immigration Check-in: add your US address, upload your I-94 (download it free at i94.cbp.dhs.gov) and an emergency contact. ISSS is in the Union Building, Room 410 · 801-581-8876.

Get your UCard

The UCard is your student ID, library card and free transit pass. The U is mobile-first: upload your photo on the UCard website (approval takes 24–48 h) and add the card to your phone. Prefer plastic? Visit the UCard Office, Olpin Union, Room 158, Mon–Fri 8 am–5 pm, with your passport.

It only works for transit once you're enrolled for the current semester.

Get a phone plan

If you're on our temporary SIM, choose your own plan before it runs out — see Phone & SIM.

Open a bank account

Deposit your cash and get a debit card. Several banks open accounts without an SSN — see banks near campus for where to go and what to bring.

TA or RA? Start your HR paperwork

I-9 in person, W-4, direct deposit and GLACIER — so your first paycheck isn't delayed. Full steps in TA / RA steps.

Apply for your Social Security Number

Needed for work. Once you've done the post-arrival check-in and are enrolled full-time, request the SSN letter through UAtlas — steps here. Office: Social Security Administration, 175 E 400 S, Suite 500 — easy by TRAX.

TB test & health portal

Book a TB test at the Student Health Center (about $10) and fill in the TB Risk Assessment in your patient portal. Upload your MMR record if you haven't.

Learn to get around

TRAX, buses, campus shuttles and SafeRide — how to use them. Then come and find us at the next event!

PhD & MS on a TA / RA

Getting set up to be paid

Your department's grad coordinator is your guide — but this is the usual order. Tap a step to open it.

1Before you arrivePre-arrival
  • Accept your offer and keep the offer letter — you'll need it for HR and the SSN.
  • Complete the ISSS pre-arrival check-in in UAtlas.
  • Register for at least 9 credits. Your tuition benefit covers up to 12 graduate credits, but your department must submit a Tuition Benefit form each semester, and you accept it in Campus Information Services — miss it and you can be charged tuition.
  • Ask your grad coordinator for the department's new-hire paperwork list and how to book your I-9 appointment.
2Check in, UCard, enrolmentDays 1–3
  • Finish the ISSS post-arrival check-in (address, I-94).
  • Meet your grad coordinator / advisor; get your UCard; confirm you're registered full-time.
  • Change your default Campus Information Services (CIS) password and log in to Employee Self Service.
3HR paperwork — I-9, W-4, direct depositBefore your start date
  • Form I-9 — in person, with original documents (photocopies aren't accepted). For F-1 students this is usually your passport + I-20 + I-94.
  • In Employee Self Service → Payroll, Taxes and Salary: fill your W-4, choose electronic W-2, and set up direct deposit to your new US account. Without direct deposit a paper cheque is mailed to your CIS address.
  • Finish any trainings your department assigns (TA training, lab safety, etc.).
Paid twice a month: 1st–15th on the 22nd, 16th–end on the 7th. A late I-9 or direct deposit pushes your first pay to the next date — see money.
4Taxes for nonresidents — GLACIERFirst weeks

As a nonresident alien you'll be invited to complete a GLACIER tax review. It decides whether a tax treaty applies (with Form 8233 if so) and sets up your FICA exemption — F-1 students are generally exempt from Social Security/Medicare tax for their first 5 calendar years, as long as you're enrolled.

Nonresident W-4s are always filed as Single with no extra allowances. Questions: University Tax Services, financialservices.utah.edu · 801-581-6976.

Every spring, all F-1 students file Form 8843 — and a 1040-NR if you earned income. ISSS shares free nonresident tax software each year.

5Social Security NumberAfter check-in & enrolment
  1. Download ISSS's On-Campus Employment Offer Letter for SSN Application. Your department / HR fills in the top part.
  2. Submit it in UAtlas (“Social Security Number Application Letter for On-Campus Employment” e-form). Processing takes up to 10 business days.
  3. When ISSS emails your umail, take the original form to ISSS (Union 410) for a signature.
  4. Fill the SS-5 application online at ssa.gov and book an appointment.
  5. Bring to the Social Security office (175 E 400 S, Suite 500): the signed letter, passport (and old passport if it holds your visa), I-20, I-94, and your SS-5 confirmation.
  6. Your card arrives by post in a few weeks — give the number to HR and your bank.
6Health insurance & TB testFirst month
  • TA/RAs on a tuition benefit get GSHIP — the Graduate School pays 100% of the single-student premium and enrols you; don't buy a plan yourself. (Spouse/children aren't subsidised.)
  • Take the TB test at the Student Health Center after you arrive.
7Keep it goingEvery semester
  • Stay enrolled (9+ credits) — your FICA exemption and tuition benefit depend on it, including in summer if you're paid.
  • RAs on grants: complete effort reporting (ePAR) when prompted each quarter.
  • Keep your I-20 signed for travel and tell ISSS when you move house.
Housing

Finding a place to live

You'll need to find your own place — we can share information, look for Bangladeshi roommates, and sometimes visit a place for you. Start early.

On campus

Cheaper and closer, but competitive — apply 3–6 months ahead, and even then it isn't guaranteed. Check the current wait times.

  • Spruce — single graduate students
  • Cedar — students with a spouse / family (very popular)
  • Cottonwood — newer family apartments, higher rent
  • University Student Apartments — all options & applying

Off campus — where to search

  • U of U Off-Campus Housing — the university's own listing site
  • KSL Homes & Rentals — Utah's local classifieds, lots of private landlords
  • Apartments.com · Zillow · Zumper · HotPads
  • FindMyPlace — student housing, linked by the U
  • Facebook: ISA at the University of Utah, University of Utah (UofU) Housing, Sublets & Roommates, and Marketplace
  • Airbnb — for a gap between arrival and your lease
The 30-minute rule. Check the public-transit time from a listing to your department on Google Maps. Don't know your building yet? Use the A. Ray Olpin University Union. Aim for 30 minutes or less.
Watch for scams. Never send a deposit for a place you haven't seen or had someone visit. Fake “landlords” on Facebook and Craigslist ask for money and send forged leases — ask us to check first. Without an SSN, some landlords may refuse you; ask if they accept your I-20 and offer letter.

Getting around

TRAX and buses are free with your UCard

UTA — TRAX, buses & more

Once you're enrolled, your UCard is your transit pass for buses, TRAX, FrontRunner, S-Line and UTA On Demand (not ski buses). Schedules and maps: rideuta.com · U of U pass details.

Tap on and tap off — every ride. On TRAX tap your UCard at the reader on the platform before boarding and again when you get off; on buses tap by the door when you get on and when you get off.

Before your UCard works, pay a regular fare. On a bus, pull the cord above the window before your stop — buses only stop when asked. TRAX stops at every station.

On campus

  • Campus shuttles — free, no tap needed. Routes and live tracking at uofubus.com or the TransLoc app. Weekdays only; limited routes after 6 pm.
  • SafeRide — a free evening ride between campus locations (weekdays, roughly 6 pm–12:30 am). Book it in TransLoc → OnDemand. Great after late lab work.
  • Ask the shuttle driver if you're unsure of your stop — they're friendly.

Handy apps: Transit (live UTA times), TransLoc (shuttles), Google Maps, Apple Maps.

What to bring

Good to know

  • Ask for a student checking account — usually no monthly fee.
  • Set up Zelle in your bank app — it's how everyone splits rent and dinner.
  • After your SSN arrives, a student or secured credit card helps build a US credit history (useful for leases later). Pay it in full each month.
  • Other options: Bank of America, Mountain America, America First, KeyBank — all banks near the U.
  • Money questions? The U's free Personal Money Management Center: 801-585-7379.
ID & driving

State ID or driver licence

A Utah ID card lets you leave your passport at home (it can't be used to drive). A Bangladeshi licence is only valid in Utah for 6 months — after that you need a Utah licence. F-1 students get a limited-term licence or ID, valid for the length of your stay.

Wait for your SSN

You need your Social Security card — or, if you aren't eligible, a letter from the Social Security office saying so.

Gather your documents

  • Passport with visa, I-94 and I-20
  • Social Security card (or ineligibility letter)
  • Two proofs of your Utah address with your name — lease, bank statement, utility bill
  • For a licence: your Bangladeshi licence or proof of a Utah driver-education course

Book an appointment

Start the application and book a slot on the Utah Driver License Division website (choose “limited-term”). Find an office — most need TRAX plus a bus.

Study & take the tests

Read the Utah Driver Handbook. At the office: eye test, photo, medical questions and the written knowledge test. The road test is often done with a certified third-party tester (driving school).

Get your card

You'll leave with a temporary paper licence; the card comes by post. Check the current fees on dld.utah.gov before you go.
